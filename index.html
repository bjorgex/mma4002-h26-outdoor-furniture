Seat 450 mm at the centre, dished to 478 at the edges — a shallow dish rather than a deep cradle. The two front planks carry an extra 11° and 4° of nose-down droop so the front edge falls away from the back of the knee, and the two rear planks are tipped 6° and 14° further down toward the back, so the seat settles into the backrest instead of rising into it. Back shaped as an S: it comes 30 mm forward of the seat rear at the lumbar (about 590 mm up), then reclines to roughly 30° at the top edge, 850 mm above ground. Each back plank is rotated to the local tangent, so the stack follows the spine rather than a flat board. Side profile 45 mm thick. The foot is not a separate choice: each side design is cast with the foot that belongs to it, so the leg and the foot are one piece of iron. The fixing hole is modelled on the ring, whose open centre is the fixing point; on skid and wishbone it would be cored in the same place. No ground fixing is shown yet. Planks 60 × 35 at 75 mm pitch measured along the curve, so every gap stays the same width instead of opening into a wedge, each rotated to the local tangent of the dish — 0° at the centre rising to about 20° at the front and rear plank — so the seat is a continuous curved surface rather than a row of flat steps, reduced at each end to a tenon with a square 30 × 30 section — the channel is square too, so the tenon cannot rock or jam as it slides, so the full section stops proud of the side and the joint reads as a shoulder, not as timber disappearing into a slot. Pitch = tenon 40 along the groove + 30 mm spacer cube = 70. Lift the bench and orbit below it to inspect the underside of the seat rail and the cored hole in each foot — the ground hides itself once the camera passes under.
